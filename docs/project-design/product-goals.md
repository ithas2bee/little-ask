# Product Goals

## 1. Product vision

Create a simple, welcoming mobile experience that empowers children to
express that they want to spend time with a parent and makes it easy for
parents to respond with care.

## 2. The problem

Children often want to do something with a parent but may hesitate to
ask. They may worry that the parent is busy, that they are interrupting,
or that the answer will be no. Parents also have moments when they want
to invite a child to share an activity but may not have a simple,
lightweight way to do so.

The product provides a clear, low-pressure way for either person to
initiate a shared moment.

## 3. Primary goal

**Give children an easy, confidence-building way to initiate quality
time with their parents.**

The child should feel that asking is welcome. The app should reduce
friction without replacing direct conversation or making connection feel
like an obligation.

## 4. Secondary goal

Allow parents to initiate simple invitations, such as: - "Want to play
catch?" - "Want to go get ice cream?" - "Want to watch a movie?" - "Want
to play a game?" - "Want to go for a bike ride?"

Parent initiation supports the two-way relationship, but it must not
displace the child-first product experience.

## 5. Product principles

### Child empowerment first

The child-facing experience is the primary design priority. It should
make initiating a request feel easy, safe, and welcome.

### Two-way connection

Both parent and child can initiate. The available actions and wording
may differ by role, but neither person is merely a passive recipient.

### Simple by design

The core workflow should be understandable without tutorials. Avoid
adding features simply because they are technically possible.

### A response is not a judgment

A parent may be unavailable. The product should communicate that clearly
and kindly, and support a suggested later time where appropriate.

### Connection, not measurement

Do not score family relationships or measure love, parenting quality, or
the number of minutes spent together.

### Privacy by default

Family requests are private to the connected family members. Do not
create public profiles, public activity feeds, or discoverability
features.

## 6. Desired outcomes

-   Children can confidently express a desire to spend time with a
    parent.
-   Parents receive clear, actionable requests.
-   Parents can respond positively or suggest another time.
-   Parents can invite children to activities.
-   Children can respond to parent invitations.
-   Both parties understand the status of a request.

## 7. MVP success signals

Evaluate the MVP qualitatively and through privacy-conscious product
feedback: - Can a child independently create and send a request? - Does
the intended parent receive and understand it? - Can the parent accept
or suggest another time? - Does the child understand the response? - Can
a parent initiate an invitation and receive a child response? - Do
families find the flow natural enough to use again?

Do not define success through streaks, competitive metrics, or pressure
to maximize usage.
